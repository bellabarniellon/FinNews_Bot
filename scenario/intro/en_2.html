Method 1. Today's market state is described by a set of features: S&P 500 and KOSPI drawdowns from their 1-year highs, the speed and volatility of the fall, VIX, and 3-month changes in the UST yield, gold, oil and the won. 2. The 50 most similar dates since 1990 are found. The model takes what every asset actually did over the next 126 trading days, so crisis co-movement between assets is preserved. 3. Equities also use a crash library of seven indices (S&P 500, KOSPI, Nasdaq, Nikkei, Hang Seng, Taiwan, Shanghai). 4. Each asset's final range blends three estimates: analogs, the crash library and plain history. The weights come from a walk-forward backtest, monthly since 2000, using only data available at each date. What the backtest showed • Ranges are calibrated: for the S&P 500 in stress months, about 85% of outcomes fell inside the 90% range, versus 67% for plain history. • On 6-month direction the model is only slightly better than plain history. Its strength is the width of the range and the tails; the median is secondary. Limits • Comparable episodes are few, especially for falls as deep as KOSPI's. • Tenge: the range comes from post-2015 history. Brent, the ruble and the won explain about half of monthly moves, and the link has weakened since 2022. A sudden devaluation is under-represented. • The model has no earnings, valuation or view on what caused the sell-off. Data: Investing.com and FRED. Not investment advice.
